# 7. Inheritance and cytonuclear interaction

Birky (2001, abstract only, doi:10.1146/annurev.genet.35.102401.090231) reviewed “The inheritance of genes in mitochondria and chloroplasts: laws, mechanisms, and models.” stating in the abstract:

> “The inheritance of mitochondrial and chloroplast genes differs from that of nuclear genes in showing vegetative segregation, uniparental inheritance, intracellular selection, and reduced recombination. Vegetative segregation and some cases of uniparental inheritance are due to stochastic replication and partitioning of organelle genomes.”

The same abstract notes that significant issues remained unresolved, “notably about the extent of paternal transmission and mitochondrial fusion in mammals.” We do not extend that mammal-specific clause to grape. Mitochondrial inheritance in *Vitis* is likewise **[待确认]** until a *Vitis* sentence is opened. A grape-specific statement of strictly maternal chloroplast inheritance is **[待确认]** unless a *Vitis* paper is quoted for it (see chapter 8 for what the grape papers actually say).

A 2026 *Journal of Experimental Botany* review (full text, doi:10.1093/jxb/erag075), “Causes and consequences of cytonuclear incompatibility in hybrids of flowering plants,” notes:

> “Hybridization and polyploidization combine divergent nuclear genomes with maternally inherited organelles, often disrupting cytonuclear coadaptation critical for respiration and photosynthesis.”

That review treats organelles as maternally inherited in the flowering-plant cases it discusses, and links cytonuclear mismatch to chlorosis, seed sterility or hybrid breakdown, and to cytoplasmic male sterility in breeding. Those are the review’s terms.

Hou et al. (JIPB 2025, full text) is the grape domestication study already cited in chapters 1 and 5: pan-mitogenome, pan-plastome, and haplotype-resolved nuclear genomes from 33 accessions, with NUMT-associated nuclear candidates including *PFD1*. They also reported “duplications of three specific genes during grapevine domestication (one *cox* and two *rpl* genes)” caused by structural variation in the mitogenome.
