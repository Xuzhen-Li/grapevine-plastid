# 8. Grapevine organelle studies

This chapter is a reading list of *Vitis* organelle papers we opened, with only those facts that appear in the text we read. Chlorotypes and mitotypes reported below are sequence types. Whether they can be read as maternal lineages in *Vitis* is **[待确认]** (chapter 7).

## Complete plastome sequence and phylogeny (2006)

Jansen et al. (2006, full text, doi:10.1186/1471-2148-6-32) sequenced the complete chloroplast genome of *Vitis vinifera* (160,928 bp; chapter 1) and used 61 protein-coding genes to place Vitaceae. The abstract says parsimony and likelihood phylogenies “provide strong support for the placement of Vitaceae as sister to the remaining rosids,” while the position of Myrtales and eurosid I monophyly depended on taxon sampling and method.

## Chloroplast population studies

Arroyo-García et al. (2006, abstract only, doi:10.1111/j.1365-294x.2006.03049.x) is titled “Multiple origins of cultivated grapevine (*Vitis vinifera* L. ssp. *sativa*) based on chloroplast DNA polymorphisms.” We have the abstract, not the full text, so haplotype counts and maps should be quoted from that abstract or from a later full text, not reconstructed from memory.

Tabidze et al. (2014, Crossref abstract, doi:10.20870/oeno-one.2014.48.2.1568), “The complete chloroplast DNA sequence of eleven grape cultivars. simultaneous resequencing methodology,” reported Illumina resequencing of eight Georgian cultivars and three French cultivars (Chardonnay, Gouais Blanc, Chasselas) “belonging to four different haplogroups (AAA, ATT, ATA, GTA),” with the Maxxa cultivar as the reference. The abstract states 100% identity of Chardonnay and Gouais Blanc, lists indel and SNP differences among haplogroups, and says “the ATT, AAA and ATA haplogroups are more closely related to each other than to the GTA haplogroup.” Full text was not obtained from EuropePMC.

Butorac et al. (2018, *PLOS ONE*, full text, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0199495; Zdunić is the last author, not the first) is titled “Assessment of wild grapevine (*Vitis vinifera* ssp. *sylvestris*) chlorotypes and accompanying woody species in the Eastern Adriatic region.” Use the full text for chlorotype calls in that region; this chapter does not copy a chlorotype table that we have not excerpted into the claim table.

An IJMS 2026 paper (full text, doi:10.3390/ijms27031583), “High-Resolution Chloroplast SNV Profiling of 409 Grapevine (*Vitis vinifera* L.) Cultivars,” reports shotgun sequencing of 409 cultivars “originating from nine countries across Southeastern and Central Europe” and, in the results:

> “A total of 93 single nucleotide variants (SNVs) were detected in 409 chloroplast genomes (Supplementary Table S3 and Figure 2).”

Sequencing was on the Ion Proton system: “422.5 million single-end reads (mean 1,033,004 reads per accession).” Three accessions (SRB-14, HRV-132 and MKD-401) “exhibited the highest number of SNVs, all sharing an identical set of 64 variants.”

Zecca et al. (2012, abstract only, doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2011.11.015) is titled “The timing and the mode of evolution of wild grapes (*Vitis*).” We did not copy divergence-time numbers from memory; quote the abstract or open the full text before using a date.

## Organelle pangenomes of 33 accessions (2025)

Hou et al. (JIPB 2025, full text) assembled mitogenomes and plastid genomes from material described in the paper first as 33 samples in four groups, then as 34 grapevine mitochondrial and plastid genomes (Tables S1, S2 and S4, read 27 November 2026, CST, from the Europe PMC supplementary file, each list the same 33 accessions and no 34th, so the “34” in that Methods sentence is unexplained) (wine, table, *V. vinifera* ssp. *sylvestris*, and *Vitis* wild relatives). They reported:

> “a pan-mitogenome (Grapepan_mt v.1.0, 1.28 Mb) (Figure 1A) and a pan-chlorogenome (Grapepan_cp v.1.0, 138 kb) (Figures S1, S2). The pan-mitogenome is about twice the size of a single mitochondrial genome, and contains 144 continuous segments from the Bmng mitochondrial genome, which serves as the reference genome in this study. The pan-chlorogenome, about the same size as a single plastid genome, contains 31 continuous segments.”

Individual mitogenomes were 663.16–817.37 kb (108–129 genes); plastomes 159,011–163,619 bp as stated in the main text (Table S4 lists 159,011–161,067 bp; see the note in chapter 1). Accessions named in the paper include PN40024, *V. vinifera* cv. Pinot Noir, and *V. vinifera* cv. Cabernet Franc as separate samples. This chapter does not describe the origin of the PN40024 nuclear reference; it only records that the JIPB authors listed PN40024 among the 33 organelle assemblies.

The JIPB sentence that continues “while TEs accounted for about 87% (Tables S4, S5)” is not taught as plastid composition in chapter 1; it remains only in that chapter’s claim table. Tables S3 and S5 were read on 27 November 2026 (CST). Plastid TE (S5): about 84% for interspersed repeats only and about 88% with all repeat classes, against “about 87%” in the text. Mitogenome TE (S3): 0.215 for interspersed repeats only and 0.223 with all repeat classes, against “about 22%”. The paper does not say which measure it used. NUMT/NUPT counts are in chapter 5.

## Other grape papers opened but used lightly

A 2020 *Scientific Reports* paper (full text, doi:10.1038/s41598-020-72799-6) reconstructs DNA-based genealogy of Nebbiolo, Barbera and other cultivars; it is not treated here as an organelle-methods paper unless a chloroplast result is quoted from its text. PLOS ONE 2023 (full text, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0283324) is “Species relationships within the genus *Vitis* based on molecular and morphological data.” A 2017 paper (abstract, doi:10.1007/s00438-017-1353-x) compares four Georgian grape cultivars at whole-genome scale, and a 2018 *BMC Plant Biology* paper (full text, doi:10.1186/s12870-018-1351-0; official title: *Genetic diversity analysis of cultivated and wild grapevine (Vitis vinifera L.) accessions around the Mediterranean basin and Central Asia*) analyses genetic diversity of cultivated and wild grapevine around the Mediterranean basin and Central Asia (the phrase “Black Sea” is not in that title or abstract); those are diversity papers, not organelle assemblers. We do not pull unreplicated numbers from them in this chapter.

Pipia et al. (2023, *Vitis* 62(4): 193–201; journal page and PDF read 27 November 2026, CST; doi:10.5073/vitis.2023.62.193-201), “Complete chloroplast genome sequencing of *Vitis vinifera* subsp. *sylvestris* – wild ancestors of cultivated grapevines,” sequenced eleven wild accessions with Illumina: eight from Europe and the Mediterranean basin and three from Georgia (Borjomi, Dusheti, Qsovrisi). The three Georgian samples were each 160,928 bp (Table 3 writes 160.928) and the AAA haplotype; most European and Mediterranean samples were GTA, and the Greek sample was ATA. The DOI printed in the PDF header uses a slash (10.5073/vitis.2023/62.193-201). That form is not registered: Crossref and doi.org return 404, while the dotted form above is registered with DataCite and doi.org redirects it to the journal page. A later Pipia et al. 2024 OENO One paper (doi:10.20870/oeno-one.2024.58.4.8087; title *Plastid genomics of wild grapevines (Vitis vinifera subsp. sylvestris) of Georgia the cradle of viticulture*) was title-matched on Crossref but it is not taught here.
