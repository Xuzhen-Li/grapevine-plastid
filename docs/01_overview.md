# 1. What plastid and plant mitochondrial genomes are

This chapter only uses statements that appear in the papers listed in the table at the end.

## Plastid genomes, with a *Vitis* example

Jansen et al. (2006) reported the complete chloroplast genome of *Vitis vinifera*. In the results section of the full text, they write:

> “The complete chloroplast genome of grape is 160,928 bp in length (Fig. 1) and includes a pair of inverted repeats 26,358 bp long, separated by a small and a large single copy region of 19,065 bp and 89,147 bp, respectively. The grape chloroplast genome has 113 unique genes, 18 of which are duplicated in the IR, for a total of 131 genes (Fig. 1).”

They also wrote that gene content and order “is identical to many other unrearranged angiosperm chloroplast genomes, including tobacco.” Figure F1 in this repository redraws that four-part layout with these numbers; it is not a reprint of their Fig. 1.

A later grape study, Hou et al. (JIPB 2025, full text) assembled plastid genomes from 33 wild and domesticated *Vitis* accessions and reported:

> “For plastid genome assembly, all 33 grape genomes were successfully assembled into the typical four-part ring structure, ranging in size from 159,011 bp (*Vitis vinifera* ssp. *sylvestris*: Vs) to 163,619 bp (*Vitis vinifera* cv. Cabernet Franc: Vcf). The average GC content of the plastid genomes was 37.38%.”

The size range and the four-part structure agree in outline with Jansen et al. The same JIPB sentence continues with “while TEs accounted for about 87% (Tables S4, S5).” Tables S4 and S5 were not in the PMC full-text XML opened for this batch, so that percentage is **not taught as plastid composition**. It is kept only in the claim table as **[待确认]** until S5 is read. It should not be read as contradicting Jansen et al.’s compact 160,928 bp plastome.

## Plant mitochondrial genomes are larger and more structurally fluid

Gualberto and Newton (2017, abstract only) summarized angiosperm mitochondrial genomes as follows:

> “The large mitochondrial genomes of angiosperms are unusually dynamic because of recombination activities involving repeated sequences. These activities generate subgenomic forms and extensive genomic variation even within the same species. Such changes in genome structure are responsible for the rapid evolution of plant mitochondrial DNA and for the variants associated with cytoplasmic male sterility and abnormal growth phenotypes.”

Wu, Liao, Zhang, Tembrock and Broz (2022, Crossref abstract only; first authors Wu et al., not “Wu, Liao, and Liu”) gave a size span from published assemblies:

> “The sizes of fully sequenced plant mitochondrial genomes span over a 100-fold range from 66 kb in *Viscum scurruloideum* to 11 000 kb in *Silene conica*. In addition to the typical circular structure, some species of plants also possess linear, and even multichromosomal, architectures.”

Sloan et al. (2012, full text) is the *Silene* paper behind that extreme. The title states “Rapid evolution of enormous, multichromosomal genomes in flowering plant mitochondria with exceptionally high mutation rates.” The abstract (from the PMC full text) should be used rather than a paraphrase of mutation-rate values that we have not copied into this chapter: if a numeric rate is needed, open that paper’s results and quote it; we do not invent one for grape.

Christensen (2014, full text) discussed why plant mitochondrial mutation and “junk” DNA need to be thought about together with repair. We do not pull unreplicated numbers from that paper here; it is listed because the full text was read as background for why assembly graphs matter.

## “One ring” is not a safe default for plant mitochondria

Kozik et al. (2019, full text), in *PLOS Genetics*, titled their paper “The alternative reality of plant mitochondrial DNA: One ring does not rule them all.” The work assembles and compares mitochondrial genomes of domesticated (*Lactuca sativa*) and wild (*L. saligna* and *L. serriola*) lettuce, interpreted as a graph of alternative structures rather than a single master circle — not *Arabidopsis*. Chapter 3 returns to this point with assembly tools.

For grape, Hou et al. (JIPB 2025, full text) reported mitochondrial assemblies across the same 33 accessions:

> “The assembled mitochondrial genomes ranged in size from 663.16 kb (*Vitis rotundifolia*: Vmu) to 817.37 kb (*Vitis vinifera* cv. Carmenere: Vca) and contained 108–129 genes. … The GC content of the mitochondrial genomes has remained relatively consistent, with an average of approximately 44%. The proportion of TEs in the mitochondrial genome is about 22% (Tables S2, S3).”

The 22% TE figure likewise sits in supplementary tables that we did not open, and is marked **[待确认]** when used as a composition fact, although the size and gene-count ranges are in the main text.
