# 9. Organelle pangenomes and population data

This is the “how these genomes are used in pangenome and population work” chapter. It does not invent a grape organelle pangenome pipeline beyond what the papers describe. Chloroplast polymorphisms used as population markers below are sequence types; reading them as maternal lineages in *Vitis* is **[待确认]** until chapter 7 is sourced.

## A published *Vitis* organelle pangenome

Hou et al. (JIPB 2025, full text) explicitly built a pan-mitogenome and a pan-plastome from 33 long-read assemblies (chapter 8): Grapepan_mt v.1.0 (1.28 Mb, about twice one mitogenome, 144 segments from the Bmng reference) and Grapepan_cp v.1.0 (138 kb, “about the same size as a single plastid genome,” 31 segments). They used those graphs together with haplotype-resolved nuclear genomes to count NUMTs and NUPTs and to run GWAS for cytonuclear interaction (chapter 5). Figure S1 of that paper is a Bandage drawing of the pan-chloroplast structure.

If a later project wants to restart a similar analysis on a public HPC, the method-level ingredients visible in that paper are: long-read organelle assembly for each accession, a union / graph representation of the 33 organelle genomes, mapping of nuclear long reads or assemblies to call NUMTs/NUPTs, and association tests. Exact software versions and parameters should be taken from that paper’s Methods, which are in the full text we opened; this chapter does not re-type an unreplicated command line. Private laboratory folders are not included here.

The Materials and Methods section of the same paper (PMC full text) names the tools actually used for that 33-accession assembly, not a general recommendation:

> “First, the long-read sequencing data were compared with the reference mitochondrial genome *Vitis vinifera* (NC_012119.1) using minimap2 v2.26 (Li, 2021), and the mapped reads were subsequently extracted to assemble the mitochondrial genome with Flye v2.9.2-b1786 (Kolmogorov et al., 2019). A similar strategy as the mitochondrial assembly was employed for assembling the plastid genomes. The pan-genomes of the grapevine mitochondrial genome and plastid genome were constructed in minigraph-0.13 (r397) with an incremental graph generation mode … Mitochondrial and plastid genomes were annotated with the Geseq tool (Tillich et al., 2017) … The forward repeats, reverse repeats and palindromic repeats within the 33 grapevine mitochondrial genomes were identified using REPuter (Kurtz et al., 2001) … NUCmer v3.1 (Kurtz et al., 2004) was used for the collinearity analysis of the 13 grape mitochondrial genomes, and the results were visualized using Mummerplot (Marçais et al., 2018).”

The population-genetic subsection of those Methods maps resequencing of 100 accessions with BWA v0.7.17-r1188, converts with SAMtools v1.13, calls variants with GATK v4.2.3.0, merges with GLnexus v1.2.7, and estimates π and *F*<sub>ST</sub> with VCFtools v0.1.16 (1,000-bp windows, 500-bp steps). Bandage appears in the Figure S1 / S2 captions (chapter 3), not in the assembly Methods paragraph quoted above.

The tool names and versions in the two Materials and Methods paragraphs quoted above were checked against the JIPB 2025 full text (PMC12498075, re-opened) and are taken verbatim from that source. They match: minimap2 v2.26; Flye v2.9.2-b1786; minigraph-0.13 (r397); the Geseq tool (Tillich et al., 2017; Methods spelling “Geseq” [sic]; the Tillich 2017 paper title uses “GeSeq”; no version number in that Methods paragraph); REPuter (Kurtz et al., 2001; no version number; the same paragraph sets “Edit distance” of 3 and “Minimal Repeat Size” of 30 bp); NUCmer v3.1; Mummerplot (Marçais et al., 2018; no version number); BWA v0.7.17-r1188; SAMtools v1.13; GATK v4.2.3.0; GLnexus v1.2.7 with the DeepVariantWGS config; and VCFtools v0.1.16 (1,000-bp windows, 500-bp steps). The same population-genetic paragraph keeps SNPs and INDELs with depths (DP) greater than 2 and maximum missing rates less than 0.1. The GWAS subsection of the same Methods uses GATK v.4.2.6.0, Fastp v.0.23.4, Picard v2.23.0 and GEMMA v.0.98.3; those versions belong to the GWAS pipeline and are not mixed into the 100-accession organelle resequencing paragraph quoted above.

The wrapper scripts in this repository print README examples. They are not grape-tuned commands, and they were not executed (UNRUN).

## Assumptions used when reading organelle pangenomes and population data

The paragraphs above describe what Hou et al. did. They are not a general *Vitis* organelle-population recipe. Analyses that treat organelle genomes as a pangenome of haplotypes, or as population markers, usually rely on the following working assumptions. They are listed so they can be checked, not asserted as established for *Vitis*:

1. **Uniparental (often maternal) inheritance.** Chapter 7 records Birky (2001, abstract) on uniparental inheritance and reduced recombination, and a 2026 *J. Exp. Bot.* review that treats flowering-plant organelles as maternally inherited in the cases it discusses. Strict maternal inheritance of plastids or mitochondria in *Vitis* is **[待确认]** (chapter 7). Chloroplast or mitochondrial types in this chapter must not be read as maternal lineages until that is sourced.

2. **Little or no recombination, so haplotypes behave as non-recombining lineages.** Birky (2001, abstract) lists “reduced recombination” among the ways organelle genes differ from nuclear genes. That is not the same as “no recombination.” Gualberto and Newton (2017, abstract; chapter 6) connect loss of nuclear repair functions to “increased ectopic recombination, genome instability, and heteroplasmy.” Woloszynska (2010, *J. Exp. Bot.*, print year 2010, online 8 December 2009; abstract; doi:10.1093/jxb/erp361) reviews plant mitochondrial heteroplasmy and writes that “Heteroplasmy is described as the evolutionary strategy of uniparentally inherited plant mitochondrial genomes which do not undergo sexual recombination,” and that “Alternative mitochondrial genomes may recombine, yielding new mtDNA variants.” Whether a given *Vitis* organelle dataset can be treated as non-recombining is **[待确认]**.

3. **Heteroplasmy is low enough to ignore, or is modelled.** Woloszynska (2010, abstract) defines heteroplasmy as “the coexistence of divergent mitochondrial genotypes in a cell,” and treats plant mitochondrial heteroplasmy as “a common situation in plants,” including a main genome plus substoichiometric sublimons. Chapter 6 records that we did not open a grape-specific heteroplasmy-frequency table; any grape heteroplasmy rate is **[待确认]**. Analyses that output one haplotype per accession assume this issue is negligible or has been handled; this chapter does not claim that.

4. **Copy-number and coverage filters in that paper belong to named subsections and must not be read as one pipeline.** The JIPB 2025 Methods (PMC12498075, already opened and re-checked) identify candidate NUMTs and NUPTs in part from sequencing depth: “the theoretical value of the depth of the NUMTs region = average depth of the nuclear genome + average depth of the mitochondrial genome,” and likewise for NUPTs with the chloroplast, then inspect HiFi reads in IGV. That NUMT/NUPT step is separate from the variant filters below.

The population-genetic subsection that computes π and *F*<sub>ST</sub> uses the “Vs” mitogenome as the reference for SNP and INDEL calling, and keeps “SNPs and INDELs with depths (DP) greater than 2 and maximum missing rates less than 0.1.” That subsection does not describe a depth cap or a NUMT filter on the variants used for π and *F*<sub>ST</sub>. Whether DP greater than 2 refers to the INFO or FORMAT field remains **[待确认]**. Because organelle coverage is usually high, that threshold may do little filtering in practice; it should not be copied as a reproduction parameter (this repository’s inference).

The same subsection calculates π and *F*<sub>ST</sub> “with a 1,000-bp sliding window and 500-bp steps.” Adjacent windows therefore overlap by half and are not independent (this repository’s note; the paper does not discuss that dependence). The paper’s criterion for selective-sweep regions is, verbatim: “the top F<sub>ST</sub> must be greater than 0.1, and the π<sub>wild</sub>/π<sub>cul</sub> ratio must be greater than 1 based on common SNPs in the grape mitogenomes.” Those cut-offs are empirical thresholds stated in the Methods; the text does not derive them.

A separate GWAS subsection of the same Methods is the only place that applies the 1.5-fold depth cap, the four-read rule, MAF ≥ 0.05, and missing < 0.2. The 1.5-fold sentence begins, verbatim, with a capital T: “To eliminate the risk of false positives arising from the presence of multiple copies, we assessed the depth of coverage for each mitochondrial variant and compared it to the average depth across the whole mitochondrial genome. Variants with coverage depths exceeding 1.5 times the mean depth were excluded (Lian et al., 2024).” The 1.5-fold threshold is cited there to Lian et al. 2024 (*Molecular Biology and Evolution*, doi:10.1093/molbev/msae028), whose Methods (read 27 November 2026, CST) say: “Sites with depth values larger than 1.5 times the average depth were discarded. In addition, mitochondrial SNPs covered by fewer than 4 reads were filtered.” The JIPB sentence therefore repeats a rule from that paper’s mitochondrial-variant filter. The same GWAS subsection “filtered out mitochondrial SNPs that were supported by fewer than four reads,” and obtained “High-quality variants … using PLINK with MAF ≥ 0.05 and missing < 0.2.” The missing-rate threshold 0.2 belongs only to this GWAS subsection; it must not be mixed with the 0.1 threshold used for π and *F*<sub>ST</sub>. The GWAS subsection uses GATK v.4.2.6.0; the 100-accession resequencing paragraph uses GATK v4.2.3.0 (already recorded above).

The statement that coverage and copy number are part of that paper’s filters is this repository’s inference: it holds for the NUMT/NUPT identification step and for the GWAS subsection as quoted above, and it must not be read as applying the GWAS depth cap to the π / *F*<sub>ST</sub> analysis.

5. **A diploid genotype caller on a haploid organelle genome.** Using a diploid genotyping model on organelle data can produce heterozygous calls that may reflect heteroplasmy or NUMTs. That reading is this repository’s inference, not a sentence in the JIPB Methods.

## Population-scale chloroplast variation

Earlier studies employed chloroplast polymorphisms as population markers: Arroyo-García et al. (2006, abstract) on the multiple origins of cultivated grapevine; Tabidze et al. (2014, abstract) on complete plastomes of eleven cultivars across four haplogroups; PLOS ONE 2018 (full text) on sylvestris chlorotypes; and IJMS 2026 (full text) on 93 SNVs across 409 cultivars (chapter 8). Those studies are population genetics with the plastome as a marker, not assemblies of a pangenome graph.

Wu et al. (2022, abstract) already noted that “only around 200 fully sequenced land plant mitochondrial genomes have been published, with many being only draft assemblies,” and called for looking at architectural variation “at the population level.” That is a review recommendation, not a grape data set.
